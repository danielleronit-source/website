If this feels familiar

You love the feeling of letting go. You want to find it sober, too.

You’re at a wedding, party, or festival. The music is good, people around you are dancing, and you want to join them—but you’re thinking about how you look, whether you seem awkward, or what to do with your body.

Maybe alcohol or another substance helps you switch off that inner commentary and finally let go. You love feeling confident, connected to the music, and free to move. You’d like to experience more of that without needing something to get you there.

Or maybe you’ve heard about ecstatic dance and feel curious, but the idea of walking into your first session feels intimidating. You’d love to feel more comfortable exploring that space, with ways to support yourself when you feel self-conscious.

That’s the wish at the heart of Permission to Dance. We explore what makes it difficult to feel that freedom, and practice ways to feel more comfortable, present, and expressive in your body.

About me

I used to need something to feel free, too.

Hi, I'm Danielle.

I'm a transformational coach and a certified IEMT practitioner. I help you work with the stuff underneath, the shame and self-consciousness that make your body tighten up on the dance floor.

I lived in that tightness for years. I needed so much alcohol or drugs just to relax at a party, let alone dance. Now I go to weddings completely sober, and people come up to me all the time to tell me how much they love the way I move.

What shifted for me was learning how to feel good in my body, and how to feel safe enough to explore. I created Permission to Dance so you can feel that freedom too. Real freedom, from the inside, without needing anything to get you there.

Read more of my storyShow less

It didn't start that way. The first time I went to a conscious festival, there was a band, and the music was so good. I was surrounded by people I felt safe with, so I could actually feel what was going on inside me. In a guided 7 chakra dance, I felt like I was on a psychedelic. That level of openness and energy, completely sober. I remember thinking, wow, this is crazy.

I've always been addicted to freedom. Drugs and alcohol used to make me feel really free. But this was the first time I felt that level of freedom without any of it. It was extremely powerful for me.

Not long after, I started traveling and ended up at an ecstatic dance in Thailand where everyone was so free. And I felt confronted. I didn't feel that free in my body or my voice. I wanted to have as much fun as everyone else looked like they were having, and it made me a bit emotional.

So I kept going back. Slowly I learned what feels good in my body, how to feel safe exploring, and how to stop caring what people think. Then I could just enjoy it.

Today I work with your subconscious to clear the mental blocks and barriers that hold you back, and I help you attune to your body so you can feel fully safe in your expression. I've spent about six years in healing communities around the world, and everything I learned there is in this program.

I created Permission to Dance because I know so many women who would love to feel this in their bodies. To have so much fun dancing, genuinely, without drugs or alcohol. It's the ultimate joy and the ultimate freedom.

I'd love to dance with you.

The two-day experience

Work with what holds you back.
Explore what freedom feels like.

Day one · Subconscious work

Work with the parts that keep you holding back.

We work with the subconscious parts of you that make it hard to feel genuinely relaxed and confident on the dance floor.

These might be the parts that worry about being judged, compare you to others, or feel they need to get everything right. We explore the feelings and beliefs they carry, making space for more ease, confidence, and freedom in your body.

Day two · Embodiment

Find your way into your body.

We bring the work into movement, using embodiment practices and tools to help you connect with sensation, notice what feels good, and follow your own rhythm.

Practice shifting your attention from how you look to the music, your sensations, and what you enjoy. The intention is to feel more genuinely comfortable dancing, with less pressure to impress or perform.

Your own rhythm

For you, if you’d love to...

  • Explore feeling free without substances. You’d like more ways to loosen up and enjoy dancing without relying on alcohol or drugs.
  • Love how you feel when dancing. Feel present, confident, and connected to the music.
  • Feel good in your body. Discover movement that feels natural to you, with room for play, pleasure, and expression.
  • Feel more ready for your first ecstatic dance. Explore movement from home and build tools to support yourself as you try a new dance space.
  • Bring that freedom into your life. Work toward enjoying a festival, party, or wedding without relying on alcohol or drugs to feel comfortable dancing.

Space to explore

Your space. Your body. Your pace.

Both sessions take place on Zoom, so you can begin from the privacy of your own home. Explore at your own pace in a familiar space, and develop tools you can take with you to your first ecstatic dance, a festival, or your next night out.
