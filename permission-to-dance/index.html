If this feels familiar

You love the feeling of letting go. You want to find it sober, too.

You’re at a wedding or a party. The music is good, your friends are dancing, and you want to join them—but you’re thinking about how you look, whether you seem awkward, or what to do with your body.

Maybe alcohol or another substance helps you switch off that inner commentary and finally let go. You love feeling confident, connected to the music, and free to move. You’d like to experience more of that without needing something to get you there.

That’s the wish at the heart of Permission to Dance. We explore what makes it difficult to feel that freedom, and practice ways to feel more comfortable, present, and expressive in your body.

The two-day experience

Work with what holds you back.
Explore what freedom feels like.

Day one · Subconscious work

Meet the blocks beneath the movement.

We explore the subconscious beliefs and emotional patterns that can make it hard to feel free on the dance floor.

That might include fear of judgment, comparing yourself to others, pressure to look attractive or get it right, or the belief that you need a drink or something else to feel confident. We get curious about what’s underneath those feelings.

Day two · Embodiment

Find your way into your body.

We bring the work into movement, using embodiment practices and tools to help you connect with sensation, notice what feels good, and follow your own rhythm.

Practice shifting your attention from how you look to the music, your sensations, and what you enjoy. The intention is to feel more genuinely comfortable dancing, with less pressure to impress or perform.

Your own rhythm

For you, if you’d love to...

  • Explore feeling free without substances. You’d like more ways to loosen up and enjoy dancing without relying on alcohol or drugs.
  • Feel less caught in how you look. Spend more time enjoying the music and less time monitoring or comparing yourself.
  • Feel good in your body. Discover movement that feels natural to you, with room for play, pleasure, and expression.
  • Bring more confidence to the dance floor. Build tools to return to yourself when self-consciousness comes up at a wedding, party, or night out.

Space to explore

Your space. Your body. Your pace.

Both sessions take place on Zoom, so you can join from the privacy of your own home. There’s room to explore this work in a familiar space, without needing to step onto a public dance floor.
